Rowan’s jaw tensed for a second, then she nodded. “Understood.”

Ethan stepped between them, holding a stack of plates. “I’ll stow these.”

Neither woman answered.

**Private Conversation: Rowan and Victoria**

Later that morning, while Ethan checked the fuel levels, Rowan lingered by the galley sink, drying her hands. Victoria entered the cabin and caught her gaze.

“Walk with me,” Victoria said simply.

They stepped out to the shaded port side walkway, wind fluttering against the sail cover above them.

“I want to clarify something,” Victoria began. “I don’t micromanage. But I do expect accuracy.”

Rowan nodded. “I wasn’t trying to be careless. I logged what I thought was relevant.”

Victoria’s voice remained level. “I don’t doubt your intellect, Rowan. I chose you for this because of it. But this yacht isn’t an academic setting. Every heading, every knot, every moment is a potential crisis if mishandled. Discipline isn’t a preference. It’s protection.”

Rowan absorbed that, then said quietly, “I understand. And I won’t let my confidence get in the way of protocol.”

Victoria gave a tight nod, then softened slightly. “Good. That’s all I needed to hear.”

Rowan tilted her head, thoughtful. “You test your crew like a behavioral psychologist, you know that?”

Victoria smirked faintly. “I don’t give tests, Rowan. The sea does. I just make sure no one fails when it matters.”

They stood there a moment longer, side by side in the sun, the wind smoothing some of the tension.

Then Victoria turned sharply. “Back to prep. We sail again tomorrow. No surprises.”

Rowan followed her back inside, thoughtful but steady.

The conversation hadn’t settled everything.

But it had established something essential: respect—with room for challenge.

And both women knew, on this boat, that was the most dangerous kind of peace.
